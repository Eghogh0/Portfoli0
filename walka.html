Case study — 01 · Walka

Walka.

walking, reimagined as a game you keep winning

01 · The problem

Fitness apps count you. They don’t move with you.

Most step apps open on a dashboard you have to go find them. The walk already happened; the app just tallies it afterwards. Motivation dies in that gap — I walked, I saw a number, I closed the app.

Walka inverts the loop. The walk is the hero: live pace, route and body feedback sit front-of-house, and the score arrives as reward rather than report. Goals adapt to the week so a quiet Tuesday doesn’t break a streak, and the social layer nudges — one friend’s route, one shared challenge — instead of performing in a feed.

The constraint that shaped everything: if the phone is in the pocket, the app still has to feel like it’s moving.

02 · Approach

Three rules, not a feature list.

  • The walk is the hero. Every screen assumes you’re mid-route, not browsing — glanceable at 40 km/h? No: glanceable at a phone-check, five seconds, one thumb.
  • Progress you can feel. Goals, streaks and pace bands react to the last seven days, not a fixed target the app picked for you.
  • A community that nudges, not a feed that scrolls. One friend’s route, one shared weekly challenge, and that’s the whole social surface.

F — 01

The walk itself

The live screen is the only screen that matters mid-walk. A route line that grows as you go, a big timer you can read with your thumb, and a bottom sheet with exactly three numbers. Nothing else competes for attention.

  • Pace, distance and heart rate — three numbers, no graphs mid-route.
  • The stop control is one thumb-reach away, at 64 px on a real phone.
  • The route line redraws in the accent brick — the app’s one warm note on a cool map.

F — 02

A goal that breathes

The daily target is a moving average of your last seven days, nudged by the season and by the weather. Slow week, slower goal; a big Saturday walks the target back up. The ring on the home screen is always close — close enough to chase, never set to fail.

  • Goal range: 60–120% of your seven-day average, capped by rest days.
  • Streaks count “kept the pace”, not “maxed the bar” — so they survive busy weeks.
  • The ring uses a 78% arc, never 100% in the mock: full circles are earned, never faked.

F — 03

Weeks, not dashboards

Insights answer the questions people actually ask — “am I doing more than last month?” — instead of showing a wall of graphs. One big number, one seven-day bar strip, two personal records. That’s the whole page.

  • The bars are honest: short walks stay short bars. No axis truncation, no cherry-picking.
  • Records only update when broken — the UI celebrates, it doesn’t re-tell history.
  • Zero-step days render a designed empty state, not a blank one.

F — 04

Wins you share, not a feed you scroll

Social is one challenge and one leaderboard, reset weekly. The point isn’t to keep looking — it’s to have a reason to stop. The leaderboard is deliberately tiny: five people, one column, no comments.

  • Challenges are capped in scope — 40 km a week, not a marathon promise.
  • “You” is the highlight row, not the top row: the app never ranks your life.
  • No infinite scroll exists anywhere in Walka. Full stop.

05 · Technology

How it’s built, and how it would be.

Architecture. React Native + TypeScript around one central reducer: the walk session. Everything — live numbers, route, state changes — flows through it, so the UI can never show a number the session doesn’t know.

Data. Daily aggregates, not raw point clouds: steps, distance and a simplified geo-polyline per walk, kept on-device first and synced to an edge Postgres (Supabase) when it matters. Privacy by default, storage kept honest.

Visual language. The same four tokens that run this whole site — ivory, ink, brick, stone — plus IBM Plex Mono for numerals and Instrument Serif for the lines that are allowed to feel human. One brick-red accent across every screen, so the product reads as one object.

06 · What I learned

Lessons that stuck.

  • Prototyping in code beats artboards. These mockups render in a browser, so the edge cases — small screens, long numbers, empty states — are already solved.
  • Data honesty. A fitness app that celebrates zero steps is dead in a week. Every screen here has an empty state designed as carefully as a full one.
  • Tokens are a contract. The same four tokens run this site and every Walka screen — that’s why they agree with each other.
  • Constraints create identity. No feeds, no infinite scroll, no streak-shaming. What you refuse to build defines the product more than what you build.
